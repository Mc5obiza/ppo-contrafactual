\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{One dataset, one system.} Every result is on RSNA Pneumonia with one generator family. We did not re-evaluate other counterfactual methods under our protocol (Supp.~E3), so the gap between CV and the anchored metrics is established for our system only.
    \item \textbf{Edits remain diffuse.} No arm we trained moved edit concentration meaningfully; every one sits in the same 0.21--0.31 band. The lock keeps at least 93\% of the change relative to the VAE round trip inside the editable lung region (Supp.~E6), so the diffuseness is within the lung. Whether diffuseness is fixable within a gradient-guidance approach is open.
    \item \textbf{n$\to$p transfer is low.} The three validators reproduce on average 28\% of the guidance shift, and \texttt{nih} stays below every threshold from 0.10 to 0.25 under both policies; the 0.15 threshold itself is not formally calibrated (\S\ref{sec:cv-overstates}).
    \item \textbf{Classifier overlap with the evaluation data.} The \texttt{rsna} guidance model and the FID extractor were trained on RSNA data, and \texttt{nih} on NIH ChestX-ray14, which contains RSNA's images (\S\ref{sec:transfer}); these models may have seen our test images, which can inflate CV and the \texttt{nih} ratio. \texttt{chex}, \texttt{pc} and \texttt{mimic\_ch} have no RSNA exposure. Generator fine-tuning, PPO rollouts and the validity probe use only the 80\% training fold.
    \item \textbf{One training run per arm.} A second PPO seed of the default configuration differs from the first by amounts of the same order as the single-vs.-ensemble gap (LHR lift $-0.117$ vs.\ $+0.189$; \S\ref{sec:five-arms}), and it is itself imperfect (shared adapter; both directions resumed from checkpoints without saved random-number state); no trained-arm gap has a proper run-to-run error bar. The single-classifier and ensemble runs also differ in classifier-selected groups (78 of 200 p$\to$n sources shared) and hardware. Three arms were evaluated with the n$\to$p adapter for both directions, which we match in their comparisons (\S\ref{sec:directional-policies}); the Grad-CAM arm's n$\to$p policy was trained for updates 1 to 49 under a different adapter from the one used at evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Adaptivity is limited} (action statistics from an evaluation replay with the policy's mean action, not read from the saved counterfactuals). In n$\to$p, 199 of 200 episodes end after one step; strength sits at its lower bound and the pass count is three on every step, guidance bias is within 1\% of its upper bound on 97.5\% of steps, and strength boost is near-constant, while the classifier and preservation scales, stop signal and ramp start vary between images. In p$\to$n the median episode is one step and the mean 5.46, 31 episodes reach the 25-step cap, and over the 71 multi-step episodes 67 to 78\% of the action variance is between images. A fixed schedule at the policy's mean action matches the policy (\S\ref{sec:main-results}), so per-image adaptation adds nothing we can detect, and a grid-tuned fixed schedule targets lesions less. The 37.9M-parameter policy was trained on at most $800 \times 25$ transitions per direction, far fewer given the short episodes.
    \item \textbf{Sample size and proxies.} The 3-model arm's p$\to$n group is small ($n=32$, 27 annotated; 12 shared for LHR). FID at $n \leq 200$ in a 1024-d space is high-variance (Supp.~E6). FOP is not computable offline, so IP measures a related property, and SCP is degenerate in this configuration.
    \item \textbf{Not measured:} convergence curves (two logged points per direction); a LoRA-rank ablation; Score-CAM or consensus masks; a wider $a_7$ range; n$\to$p lesion targeting (LHR needs annotated sources). The lock width was not tuned (exploratory check in Supp.~E4).
\end{itemize}
