Counterfactual explanations for image classifiers are usually judged by validity: how often an edit flips the decision of the classifier that guided it. Because the same model steers and scores the edit, high validity cannot distinguish a class-relevant change from an adversarial perturbation. We propose an anchored evaluation protocol for chest radiograph counterfactuals that pairs validity with two measurements, each calibrated against explicit nulls: a held-out transfer ratio, which re-scores counterfactuals with classifiers never used for guidance and is bracketed by an adversarial-attack floor and a real-image ceiling, and an in-lung Lesion Hit Rate against radiologist-drawn opacity boxes. We apply it on RSNA Pneumonia to a PPO-scheduled latent diffusion generator (PPO-CF) and to an iterative Latent Shift baseline. Normal-to-pneumonia counterfactuals reach 94.5% validity, yet three held-out classifiers reproduce only 28% of the guidance shift, no more than a norm-matched attack on the guidance models; Latent Shift behaves the same way. Pneumonia-to-normal edits, the direction with annotated lesions, transfer well above the attack floor and do target lesions, but weakly (1.16$\times$ chance within the lung, about half of it explained by where lesions typically lie). A whole-image null would score them 2.6$\times$, below the 3.2$\times$ of an edit spread uniformly over the lung. A fixed schedule at the policy's mean action matches the learned policy in validity and lesion targeting, so the controller contributes an aggressive schedule rather than per-image adaptation. Validity should be reported only with such anchored, null-calibrated checks.
