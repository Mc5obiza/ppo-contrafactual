\chapter{Presentation of the Tasks Performed}
\label{ch:tasks}

This chapter details the technical work carried out during the
internship: the architecture of the research pipeline, the construction
of each of its modules, and --- occupying the larger part of the
internship --- the successive redesign cycles that experimental evidence
forced upon it.

\section{Global Pipeline Architecture}

\subsection{Delivered Functional Flow}

The system is organised as a single linear data flow, split across two
independent stages that share a common construction step:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The RSNA dataset is downloaded and exposed as a structured,
        indexable dataset object.
  \item A shared construction routine builds the dataset, the frozen
        models and the generator. Both training and evaluation call it,
        which guarantees they operate on \emph{identical} objects.
  \item The classifier, the PSPNet segmenter and the YOLOv8 feature
        extractor are loaded and frozen.
  \item The Stable Diffusion UNet is adapted to the radiograph domain via
        LoRA.
  \item Two directional PPO policies are trained against that adapted
        generator.
  \item Evaluation reloads the checkpoints and computes the full metric
        suite and the qualitative diff visualisations.
\end{enumerate}

Alongside this main path sit two diagnostic tools that never participate
in training: the held-out validation procedure, and a classifier
localisation check, both described below.

\subsection{Splitting Training from Evaluation}
\label{sec:walltime}

The pipeline was originally a single process that trained both policies
and then evaluated them. This design failed concretely: one early run
completed PPO training, wrote both policies to disk, and was then killed
at the 24-hour walltime boundary partway through evaluation. No metrics
were produced, and --- critically --- no entry point existed that could
reload the surviving policies. A full training run had to be repeated to
recover a number that the finished policies could already have produced.

The pipeline was restructured in response. Training now terminates as
soon as checkpoints are written; evaluation runs as a separate process,
failing fast with an explicit message if the expected policy checkpoints
are absent. Beyond removing the walltime hazard, this gives evaluation
the property of being freely repeatable: metric fixes, different sample
sizes and regenerated plots no longer cost a training run.

This split also created a subtler risk: with training and evaluation in
one process, a checkpoint that fails to reload correctly in a \emph{cold}
process is invisible, because the objects are already in memory. A
dedicated evaluation smoke test was added specifically to exercise that
cold-reload path.

\section{Data and Generator Work}

\subsection{Dataset Preparation}

The RSNA Pneumonia Detection Challenge corpus is retrieved and a binary
label derived from the challenge's bounding-box annotations: a study is
positive if any box marks lung opacity, negative otherwise. DICOM files
are decoded, min--max normalised to $[0,1]$ and resized to
$256\times256$. The loaded corpus comprises \textbf{26{,}684 studies}:
6{,}012 positive and 20{,}672 negative.

\subsection{Class Imbalance and Balanced Sampling}

The $22.5\%$ positive rate is a problem for denoising fine-tuning, which
is class-conditional: trained on the natural distribution, the generator
sees pneumonia-positive radiographs less than a quarter of the time and
learns a correspondingly weaker conditional model for exactly the class
the harder edit direction must synthesise.

Fine-tuning addresses this with a weighted random sampler, weighting each
sample by the inverse frequency of its class so that each epoch presents
roughly balanced classes. The sampler fails loudly rather than silently
degrading if a class is absent from the dataset.

\subsection{LoRA Fine-Tuning}

The SD1.5 UNet is adapted with rank-16 LoRA adapters ($\alpha = 16$,
dropout $0.05$) inserted into the attention layers' query, key, value
and output projections, trained on the standard $\epsilon$-prediction
denoising objective for 30 epochs at learning rate $10^{-4}$. Class
conditioning replaces SD1.5's text encoder entirely: a small projection
network maps the target class and a pooled latent summary to a single
cross-attention conditioning token.

The training loop checkpoints every epoch and resumes from its last
saved state, a necessity in a walltime-bounded batch environment --- a
run resumed after a completed 30-epoch fine-tune correctly detects that
and skips straight to the next stage.

An earlier iteration used rank~8; its checkpoints were preserved
separately for comparison.

\subsection{Gradient-Guided DDIM Sampling}

Counterfactual generation performs a partial forward-then-reverse DDIM
trajectory, whose length is set by a strength fraction of the
$T_{\text{diff}}=50$ step schedule. At each reverse step, two gradient
terms are injected into the predicted clean latent $\hat z_0$:

\[
  \Delta z_0 = -\,w(i)\,\alpha_{\text{cls}}\,
  \nabla_{\hat z_0}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}
  \;-\; w(i)\,\alpha_{\text{pres}}\,\nabla_{\hat z_0}
  \big(\text{seg-weighted L1} + \eta\,\text{obj-det MSE}\big)
\]

The first term drives the classifier toward the target class; the second
penalises departure from the original image, both in anatomy
(segmentation-weighted L1) and in object-detector feature space. The
factor $w(i)$ is a linear ramp that rises from 0 to 1 starting at a
policy-chosen fraction of the trajectory, replacing an earlier hard
on/off guidance switch whose discontinuity destabilised sampling.

\subsection{The Background Lock}

Gradient guidance alone provides no guarantee about \emph{where} an edit
lands. The generator therefore applies a hard spatial constraint adapted
from RePaint~\cite{lugmayr2022repaint} and Blended Latent
Diffusion~\cite{avrahami2022blended}: at every denoising step, the latent
\emph{outside} a dilated, Gaussian-feathered lung-segmentation mask is
overwritten with the original image's latent, forward-noised to the
current timestep (and clean at the final step).

This is a structural guarantee rather than a soft penalty. Content
outside the lung field cannot be repainted by the sampler regardless of
how the guidance gradient behaves; it can only collapse to the VAE's
reconstruction floor. Without it, an aggressive sampling schedule
repaints the entire thorax.

\section{Reinforcement-Learning Control}

\subsection{Environment Design}

The sampling procedure is exposed as a Gym-style MDP. An episode begins
by sampling a ``far'' image --- one the classifier scores more than
$0.5$ away from the target class. \textbf{15{,}267 of 26{,}684 images}
qualify for the \ntop{} direction, split into 15{,}243 for rollouts and
24 withheld for a validity probe.

The observation concatenates the flattened original and current latents,
a normalised step index, the current $|f(x_t) - c|$, the target class,
and the flattened lung mask --- $73{,}731$ dimensions in total. Episodes
terminate when $|f(x_t) - c| < \varepsilon_{\text{stop}} = 0.08$ or the
step budget $T = 8$ is exhausted.

\subsection{Action Space}

The policy emits a 9-dimensional continuous action at each step, mapped
through sigmoid transforms onto bounded control ranges:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption[PPO action space]{PPO action space.}
\label{tab:actions}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dim} & \textbf{Controls} & \textbf{Range} \\
\midrule
$a_0$ & DDIM strength                                  & $[0.20,\,0.70]$ \\
$a_1$ & classifier-guidance scale                      & $[0.30,\,1.20]$ \\
$a_2$ & preservation-guidance scale                    & $[0.30,\,1.50]$ \\
$a_3$ & stop signal $\tau_t$                           & $[0,\,1]$ \\
$a_4, a_5$ & one extra refinement pass each (Bernoulli gates) & $\{0,1\}$ \\
$a_6$ & per-pass strength boost                        & $[0.85,\,1.15]$ \\
$a_7$ & guidance bias (classifier vs.\ preservation)   & $[0.70,\,1.30]$ \\
$a_8$ & guidance ramp-up start fraction                & $[0.10,\,0.50]$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The classifier-guidance ceiling in $a_1$ was deliberately narrowed during
development, from a maximum of $3.0$ down to $1.2$. At the original
ceiling a single step could brute-force the classifier flip, and episodes
collapsed to an average length of exactly $1.00$ --- leaving PPO with no
temporal structure from which to learn credit assignment. Capping the
per-step push forces the flip to be reached over several steps, which is
what makes the problem a sequential decision problem at all.

\subsection{Actor-Critic Architecture}

The policy network uses a convolutional trunk over the latent-shaped
portion of the observation (three convolutional layers, $32\to64\to128$
channels, followed by a 256-unit MLP head that also consumes the scalar
observation components). This trunk feeds four heads: a Gaussian policy
head with a \textbf{learnable per-dimension standard deviation}
parameter, a value head, and three auxiliary heads predicting
feasibility, progress and the stop decision, each trained with its own
auxiliary loss.

Making the policy's standard deviation a learned parameter rather than a
fixed schedule lets the agent modulate its own exploration per action
dimension. The resulting policy is compact: \textbf{197{,}910
parameters}, against an observation of $73{,}731$ dimensions.

Advantages are computed with GAE~\cite{schulman2016gae}
($\gamma = 0.99$, $\lambda = 0.95$) and the policy updated with the
clipped PPO objective~\cite{schulman2017ppo} (clip $0.2$, value
coefficient $0.5$, entropy coefficient $0.01$).

\subsection{Two Directional Policies}

The environment is constructed around a single fixed target class, so two
policies are trained independently: one for normal$\rightarrow$pneumonia
($c=1$) and one for pneumonia$\rightarrow$normal ($c=0$). They share the
same fine-tuned generator but specialise their sampling schedules. A
single target-conditioned policy would be architecturally cleaner, and is
identified in Section~\ref{sec:future} as future work.

\section{Iterative Debugging and Redesign}

The chronology below is reconstructed from the project's development
history and from the design rationale recorded alongside the system's
configuration, which was maintained as a running experimental log
throughout the internship.

\subsection{Diagnosis: the Reward Was Almost Entirely a Flip Bonus}

Early counterfactuals changed between 66\% and 75\% of all pixels ---
diffuse, whole-image edits rather than localised lesions. Decomposing the
reward actually obtained by the first policy version, whose episodes
averaged length $1.00$ with a mean return of $33.931$, explained why:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption[v1 reward decomposition]{Reward decomposition of the first policy
version. The terminal flip bonuses accounted for 93\% of the return;
preservation was numerically negligible.}
\label{tab:v1reward}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Reward term} & \textbf{Contribution} & \textbf{Share} \\
\midrule
\Rterm{hit} $\times 6$ and \Rterm{stop} $\times 8$ & $+14.00$ & 93\% \\
\Rterm{prog} $\times 1.5$                          & $+1.40$  & 9\% \\
\Rterm{pres} $\times 0.5$                          & $-0.21$  & $-1.4$\% \\
\Rterm{fid} $\times 0.5$ (on MSE $0.0033$)         & $-0.0016$& $-0.01$\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The flip bonus \emph{was} the reward. Preservation contributed roughly
one part in a hundred and image fidelity one part in ten thousand, so the
optimal policy was to flip the classifier by any means available and stop
immediately. Three changes followed:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Rebalancing.} The terminal bonuses were cut
        ($\lambda_{\text{hit}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{stop}}$ from 6 and 8
        down to 2 each) and the preservation weight raised
        ($\lambda_{\text{pres}}$ from 0.5 to 3.0), targeting a roughly
        40/40/20 split between flip, preservation and progress.
  \item \textbf{L2 $\rightarrow$ L1 fidelity penalty.} \Rterm{fid} was
        computed as a mean squared error. L2 prefers many small changes
        over few large ones --- it was \emph{actively rewarding} the
        diffuse failure mode being diagnosed. Switching to L1, which is
        sparsity-inducing, required rescaling $\lambda_{\text{fid}}$ from
        0.5 to 2.0, since L1 is roughly $25\times$ larger numerically at
        these magnitudes.
  \item \textbf{Episode-origin anchoring.} The background lock and the
        preservation targets were changed to anchor to the episode's
        \emph{original} image rather than to the previous refinement
        pass's output. Anchoring to the previous pass allows unbounded
        drift over an episode; anchoring to the origin restores the
        Markov property and forces the policy to refine rather than to
        blast.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A Reward-Scaling Bug in the Asymmetry Correction}

The \ntop{} direction is empirically harder than \pton{}, and was
originally compensated with a $2.25\times$ boost applied to its reward.
The scale was found to be applied only to the progress terms and not to
\Rterm{fid} or \Rterm{pres} --- meaning preservation was effectively
$2.25\times$ \emph{less} important on precisely the direction that was
already producing the most diffuse edits.

Applying the scale uniformly across all reward terms corrected the
relative weighting. It is worth stating explicitly that under PPO's
per-update advantage normalisation a uniform scalar multiplying the
entire reward is close to a no-op for the policy gradient. The fix
removed a harmful \emph{relative} re-weighting; it did not inject
additional learning signal into the harder direction, and the residual
asymmetry in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} is consistent with that.

\subsection{Grad-CAM Localisation Check}
\label{sec:gradcam_mask}

Before trusting a classifier to supply a guidance gradient, it is
necessary to establish that it responds to pathology rather than to a
global brightness or contrast shortcut --- a well-documented failure mode
of CXR classifiers, and one that would make every generated
counterfactual a mere exposure adjustment.

A dedicated diagnostic renders Grad-CAM overlays for this purpose. The
comparison between an earlier in-house classifier and the TorchXRayVision
DenseNet-121 ultimately adopted as the guidance model is shown in

The same Grad-CAM implementation was subsequently promoted from a
diagnostic to a \emph{constraint}. Once enabled, the map is normalised at
its 99th percentile, raised to a power $\gamma = 0.75$, floored at $0.15$
and Gaussian-blurred, then used to modulate the edit budget spatially ---
so the agent may edit freely where the classifier attends and only
marginally elsewhere.

Enabling this required an ensemble-aware Grad-CAM path. Without it,
resolving the target layer on an ensemble silently returns the \emph{first
member's} feature block, producing a single-model CAM mislabelled as the
ensemble's --- verified behaviour, recorded as such precisely so it could
not be mistaken for a suspicion later.

\subsection{Sparsity Reward with Annealed Ramp}

Even after the reward rebalance, measured edit concentration --- the
share of total $|\Delta|$ carried by the top 5\% of pixels --- stood at
$0.282$ (\ntop{}) and $0.297$ (\pton{}) against a uniform baseline of
$0.05$. A concentrated core existed, but most of the edit mass sat in a
diffuse tail.

An explicit L1 sparsity reward term was added to target that tail, with
two refinements. First, it is \emph{annealed}: the weight is zero for the
first 20\% of updates, ramps linearly to its target of $2.0$ between 20\%
and 60\%, and is constant thereafter, so the policy first learns a
reliable flip and only then is pressured toward minimal edits. Second,
the penalty can be restricted to weight only edit mass \emph{outside} the
Grad-CAM-attended region, since a plain, unweighted penalty would also
penalise the concentrated core the method is trying to keep.

A safeguard accompanies the ramp: validity is probed on a held-out set
every 10 updates, and the ramp freezes if it drops more than 8 points
below its pre-ramp baseline. The size of that held-out set was chosen
rather than guessed. Validity is a fraction over $N$ images, so its
resolution is $1/N$; at $N=8$ a single image moves it by 12.5 points and
the freeze would latch on noise in essentially every run. $N=24$ gives
4.2 points per image, placing the 8-point tolerance safely above the
noise floor.

\subsection{Held-Out Validation and the Move to an Ensemble}
\label{sec:heldout_method}

The most consequential finding of the internship came from questioning
the primary metric. Counterfactual Validity was being computed with the
\emph{same} classifier that produced the guidance gradient. That number is
circular: it measures how well an optimiser optimised its own objective,
not whether the resulting image depicts disease.

A held-out validation procedure was written as the independent check. It
re-scores the generated counterfactuals with TorchXRayVision classifiers
that never participated in the guidance loop, and includes an agreement
control on the \emph{original} images --- so that poor transfer cannot be
dismissed as the validators simply disagreeing about the task. It runs
deliberately as a separate step from the main evaluation pipeline, and
caches its generated counterfactuals so that adding a validator costs
minutes rather than hours.

The results (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, Section~\ref{sec:heldout}) were
negative, and led directly to a redesign: the guidance signal was changed
from a single classifier trained on RSNA data to an \textbf{ensemble} of
that classifier and one trained on CheXpert. The reasoning is that an
adversarial direction found against one model rarely survives against an
independently trained one, so requiring consensus makes the adversarial
shortcut a substantially narrower target. The CheXpert-trained model was
chosen specifically because its eleven-head multi-task training resists
the global-brightness shortcut.

This change carries a bookkeeping obligation: any classifier in the
guidance ensemble ceases to be a valid held-out validator. The validator
set was accordingly reduced to three classifiers trained on NIH, MIMIC-CXR
and PadChest data respectively.

\section{Experimental Infrastructure}

\subsection{Smoke Tests}

Dedicated smoke tests run the training and evaluation paths end-to-end
with deliberately tiny budgets, to surface crashes and tensor-shape
errors before a 20-hour job is queued. The training smoke test is
parameterised to select between a baseline configuration (all new flags
off, reproducing the pre-change pipeline), a Grad-CAM-constraint-only
configuration, or the full configuration --- the same ablation arms used
to attribute behaviour changes to individual mechanisms.

\subsection{Job Monitoring}

Every batch job is configured to send a notification on completion or
failure, so job status is reported without polling. Each job also
performs explicit precondition checks before loading any model weights
--- the evaluation and held-out procedures verify that the expected
policy checkpoints exist, and the held-out procedure additionally
verifies that each validator's weights are present in the local
TorchXRayVision cache, failing in seconds rather than after three hours
of generation.

One such check encodes a non-obvious detail worth recording: the
TorchXRayVision cache filename does not always match the weight
identifier, because the RSNA-trained classifier is cached under a name
referencing Kaggle rather than RSNA (the dataset's original hosting
platform), so a naive existence check on the more obvious name fails even
on a correctly provisioned machine.

Every process runs unbuffered, so that a job's log is readable while it
is still running. In a non-interactive environment where a stalled job
cannot be inspected directly, the log is the only diagnostic channel
available.

\section{Conclusion}

This chapter has described the construction of the research pipeline and
the sequence of redesigns that experimental evidence forced: a reward
function found to be 93\% flip bonus, a fidelity penalty that rewarded
the failure mode it was meant to suppress, a direction-scaling bug that
penalised preservation on the harder direction, and --- most importantly
--- a primary metric found to be circular. The next chapter reports what
the resulting system measures, and what those measurements do and do not
establish.
