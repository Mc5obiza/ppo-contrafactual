\chapter{Additional Work: Extending to a Second Domain}
\label{ch:breast}

Everything reported up to this point concerns a single dataset and a single
pathology: binary pneumonia classification on RSNA chest radiographs. This
short chapter documents work that sits outside the six work packages of
Chapter~\ref{ch:context} --- a self-initiated extension, undertaken once the
core RSNA pipeline and its held-out-validation apparatus were in place, to
test whether the method and, more importantly, its \emph{evaluation
discipline} generalise beyond chest radiography at all. It should be read as
exactly that: additional, exploratory work, reported with the same honesty
as Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, not as a second fully-validated result standing
alongside the main one.

\section{Motivation}

Section~\ref{sec:heldout} established the internship's central lesson: a
counterfactual system can report near-perfect validity against the
classifier that guided it while having learned almost nothing about the
target pathology. That lesson is only as strong as its generality. A single
success --- or, here, a single negative result --- on one dataset, one
modality and one pretrained model zoo leaves open the possibility that the
finding was an artefact of RSNA, of TorchXRayVision, or of pneumonia
specifically.

Mammography was chosen as the second domain precisely because it shares as
little as possible with the first. It is a different imaging modality, it
has no representation at all in TorchXRayVision's eighteen pathology
labels, and its natural binary task --- malignant versus benign lesion ---
is semantically different from RSNA's healthy-versus-diseased framing, as
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:breast_semantics}. If the pipeline's
architecture and its habit of distrusting its own validity number survive a
change this large, that is much stronger evidence than a second run on a
second chest-radiograph corpus would have been.

\section{A Domain-Registry Architecture}

Before this extension, three things that logically belong together were
scattered across unrelated files: the dataset and its label, the pathology
head the classifier guidance targets, and which clinical metrics are even
meaningful for that pathology. Changing the dataset without also changing
the other two silently produces nonsense --- guidance toward the wrong
disease, or a cardio-thoracic ratio reported for an anatomy that has no
heart in frame.

\file{domains/base.py} introduces a \texttt{DomainProfile} dataclass that
binds all three together, together with a set of optional
\texttt{build\_*} hooks (\texttt{build\_guidance}, \texttt{build\_validators},
\texttt{build\_masker}, \texttt{build\_lesion\_net}) that a domain may
override when the TorchXRayVision-shaped defaults --- an XRV classifier head,
a PSPNet segmenter, CTR/SCP as the clinical metrics --- do not apply.
Left unset, a domain behaves exactly as the original single-dataset pipeline
did, which is what keeps the RSNA domain module byte-identical to the
pre-refactor behaviour. Adding a new dataset is then one new module in
\file{domains/} plus one line in \file{config.py}, rather than an edit
threaded through ingestion, guidance and evaluation code.

\section{What Mammography Required Rebuilding}

Almost nothing about the TorchXRayVision-based guidance stack survives the
move to mammography, because that whole stack --- the pathology classifier,
the PSPNet lung/heart segmenter, and the FID feature extractor --- has no
breast equivalent. \file{domains/cbis_ddsm_breast.py} therefore supplies
its own version of every frozen component the RSNA pipeline was able to
borrow for free.

\subsection{A classifier trained from scratch}

\file{train_breast_classifier.py} trains $f(\cdot)$ for this domain, since no
pretrained breast-malignancy head exists in the model zoo used elsewhere in
this project. Three backbones are trained under \texttt{config.BREAST\_BACKBONES}
(\texttt{densenet121}, \texttt{efficientnet\_b0}~\cite{tan2019efficientnet},
\texttt{resnet18}); the
first two form the guidance ensemble and the third is held back as the
independent validator, mirroring the guidance/validator split described in
Section~\ref{sec:heldout_method} for the chest domain. \texttt{run\_breast\_classifier\_gate.slurm}
applies a hard floor (\texttt{MIN\_ACCEPTABLE\_AUC}$=0.65$) against each
backbone's saved validation AUC before any of them is allowed to guide
generation, failing the job explicitly rather than proceeding with a
near-chance classifier.

The split is taken at the \emph{patient} level, not the image level. CBIS-DDSM
contains up to four images per patient (left/right breast, CC/MLO view) plus
one row per lesion; a random image-level split would place the same breast
in both train and validation and report a memorisation score as an AUC.

\subsection{Two segmenters instead of one}

The chest pipeline's single PSPNet segmenter conflated two roles that
mammography must separate. \file{src/breast_segmentation.py} supplies both:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{BreastAnatomySegmenter}}, an analytic (not learned)
        segmenter producing breast/pectoral/dense-tissue masks, used to gate
        the background lock exactly as the lung mask does for RSNA.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{LesionSegmenter}}, a U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet}
        trained by \file{train_lesion_segmenter.py} on CBIS-DDSM's shipped
        region-of-interest masks. It is deliberately excluded from the
        guidance loop, so that the clinical metrics computed from it stay
        independent of the edits they are measuring --- the same
        independence principle that motivated held-out validation in the
        first place.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A new clinical metric suite}

CTR and SCP, the chest domain's clinical proxy metrics, have no meaning
here: there is no heart or costophrenic recess in a mammogram.
\file{src/metrics.py} adds four mammography-specific metrics instead.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{LLR (Lesion Load Ratio)} --- lesion area over breast area,
        the structural analogue of CTR. Benign$\to$malignant edits are
        expected to raise it; malignant$\to$benign edits to lower it.
  \item \textbf{MSI (Margin Spiculation Index)} --- normalised boundary
        complexity (perimeter$^2$ / $4\pi\cdot$area) of the segmented
        lesion's largest component. Margin morphology is the single most
        load-bearing malignancy sign in mammography, and CBIS-DDSM labels it
        directly (\texttt{mass margins}: spiculated/ill-defined versus
        circumscribed), which makes MSI \emph{calibratable on real images}
        in a way SCP never was. \file{check_lesion_localization.py} is the
        gate: MSI must separate spiculated from circumscribed lesions on
        ground-truth images before it may be reported as evidence about
        counterfactuals at all. This calibration script was written and is
        part of the delivered pipeline, but was not run to completion within
        the time available for this additional work, so the MSI numbers in
        Section~\ref{sec:breast_results} should be read as provisional
        pending that gate.
  \item \textbf{BDC (Breast Density Contrast)} --- mean intensity of the
        segmented dense (fibroglandular) tissue. This one is a diagnostic
        rather than a success criterion, purpose-built to catch a specific
        failure this project had already seen once, on RSNA: job
        \textbf{17648985} reported CV$=0.975$ while changing 73.6\% of all
        pixels at MAE $=0.036$ --- a diffuse global brightness wash that
        moved the classifier without editing any anatomy. The mammography
        signature of the same failure is a large BDC shift paired with a
        flat LLR: the generator brightened or darkened the whole breast
        rather than altering a lesion. Section~\ref{sec:breast_results}
        reports exactly this pattern.
  \item \textbf{MOP (Marker/clip Preservation)} --- the mammography
        counterpart of FOP, and, unlike FOP, computable offline: surgical
        clips and skin markers are small, saturated, high-contrast metal
        objects that a simple blob detector can find without a trained
        model, sidestepping FOP's dependency on a device detector that does
        not exist for this project.
\end{itemize}

\section{Dataset and Label Semantics}
\label{sec:breast_semantics}

CBIS-DDSM (Curated Breast Imaging Subset of DDSM)~\cite{lee2017cbisddsm} is retrieved from a public
Kaggle mirror by the same \texttt{setup\_env.sh} provisioning step used for
RSNA. It comprises roughly 3{,}500 mass and calcification lesion images,
pooled across both abnormality types, with a workable class balance of
approximately 1{,}400 malignant against 2{,}100 benign or
benign-without-callback lesions --- a considerably less skewed split than
RSNA's 22.5\% positive rate.

One property of the dataset changes what the resulting counterfactuals mean.
CBIS-DDSM is a curated subset of \emph{lesion} cases: every image in it
contains an abnormality, and there are no normal mammograms at all. The
binary label is therefore \textbf{malignant versus benign}, not
healthy versus diseased, and the two PPO directions are
benign$\to$malignant and malignant$\to$benign. This is stated explicitly
because it is easy to misread: a reader carrying over the chest framing
might read a successful malignant$\to$benign edit as ``the model removed
the cancer,'' when what it actually claims is the narrower, weaker
statement that the generator made an already-present lesion look benign to
the classifier.

The guidance diagnostics recorded alongside each policy checkpoint
(\file{guidance\_diagnostics\_\{n\_to\_p,p\_to\_n\}.json}, a 20-episode probe)
show the same kind of directional specialisation observed on RSNA: the
benign$\to$malignant policy settles at a higher guidance bias (mean $0.980$,
$3.45$ refinement passes on average, average reward $8.53$) than the
malignant$\to$benign policy ($0.790$, $3.20$ passes, average reward $5.10$),
indicating the two directions again learned visibly different control
strategies rather than converging to a shared, direction-agnostic schedule.

\subsection{Reading These Numbers Honestly}

Three things in these tables should stop a reader before they are taken as a
mammography success story.

\textbf{The CV number is exactly the shape of the number that misled the
project once already.} A mean Counterfactual Validity of $1.000$ against the
guidance ensemble is, superficially, an even stronger result than RSNA's
$0.975$. Section~\ref{sec:heldout} showed that a comparably high number
computed against a guidance classifier collapsed to at most 8\% under
genuinely independent validators. No held-out re-scoring was run for this
domain within the time available for this additional work, so this CV
figure carries the same circularity concern as RSNA's uncorrected number did,
and should not be read as evidence of validity on its own. It is reported
here for completeness, not as a result.

\textbf{Even if it were run, the held-out check here would be weaker than
RSNA's.} The RSNA ensemble's members and validators were trained by
different research groups on different hospitals' data. This domain's three
backbones --- the guidance pair and the held-out validator --- were all
trained by this project, from the same $\sim$3{,}500-image dataset, and
differ only in network architecture. Any transfer ratio a future held-out
run produces here would be an optimistic upper bound relative to the chest
result, not a directly comparable number.

\textbf{The BDC/LLR pairing already reproduces the RSNA failure signature,
without needing a held-out classifier to find it.} In the malignant
$\to$benign direction, BDC shifts significantly ($t=2.46$, $p=0.015$) while
LLR --- the metric that would move if a lesion were actually shrinking or
disappearing --- does not ($p=0.732$). That is precisely the pattern BDC was
designed to catch: a diffuse change in overall breast density large enough
to move the classifier, occurring while the segmented lesion's relative size
is statistically unchanged. Read together with an edit-concentration figure
well below what a focal, lesion-confined edit would produce (0.33--0.37
against a uniform baseline of $0.05$, with 38--41\% of all pixels changed),
the most defensible reading of this domain's results is that the system has,
at least in part, reproduced on mammography the same adversarial shortcut
that the held-out validation exposed on chest radiographs --- caught this
time by a purpose-built diagnostic metric rather than by an independent
classifier, because that classifier was never trained.

\section{What This Additional Work Actually Demonstrates}

This extension does not add a second validated success to the internship's
results. What it does demonstrate is narrower and, in the context of this
report's central methodological argument, more useful: the domain-registry
architecture generalises cleanly to a modality that shares essentially
nothing with chest radiography, and the discipline of building an
independent check \emph{before} trusting a validity number generalises with
it. The BDC diagnostic --- designed specifically because a diffuse-edit
failure had already been caught once on RSNA --- found the same qualitative
pattern here without a single line of the guidance pipeline being reused.
That the lesson transferred even though almost none of the code
(classifier, segmenters, metrics) did is, if anything, a stronger argument
for the internship's methodological contribution than a second clean
success would have been.

Completing the held-out validation for this domain --- training the third
validator backbone was already budgeted for exactly this purpose --- and
running the MSI ground-truth calibration gate are the two immediate next
steps, and are listed alongside the chest-domain future work in
Section~\ref{sec:future}.
