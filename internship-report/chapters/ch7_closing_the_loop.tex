\chapter{Closing the Loop: Real Ensemble Training and Full Re-Validation}
\label{ch:closing}

Chapter~\ref{ch:integrity} closed on two explicitly unresolved items,
repeated in Section~\ref{sec:future}: whether the trained/random gap in
Table~\ref{tab:lhr_arms} survives a paired per-image test, and whether
ensemble guidance --- shown in that chapter only on no-policy, generation-only
arms (\texttt{cm1}, \texttt{cm4}) --- actually closes the held-out transfer
gap of Table~\ref{tab:heldout} when a policy is \emph{trained under it}
rather than merely evaluated through it. This short chapter closes both, in
a later work session after the chapters above were written.

\section{Making Ensemble Guidance the Trained Default}

\file{config.py}'s \texttt{USE\_GUIDANCE\_ENSEMBLE} flag had been reverted to
\texttt{False} on 2026-08-07 for comparability with the archived
\texttt{v1\_rank8} policies, meaning every trained-policy result in
Chapters~\ref{ch:results}--\ref{ch:integrity} --- including
Table~\ref{tab:heldout} and the trained arm of Table~\ref{tab:lhr_arms} ---
was produced under single-classifier guidance, even though
Section~\ref{sec:arm_results} had already shown ensemble
guidance to be the one countermeasure that helps. This was corrected: the
flag now defaults to \texttt{True}, with the previous behaviour still
reachable by setting it back to \texttt{False}.

Flipping a config default is not, by itself, evidence of anything --- the
trained policies on disk still reflected the old value until retrained. Two
checks were needed before that retraining could be trusted:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The resume path silently no-ops on stale state.}
        \texttt{PPOTrainer} resumes from \texttt{ppo\_latest.pt} and, on
        finding the target episode count already reached, prints
        ``nothing to do'' and re-saves the checkpoint files without
        training a single new step. A first smoke-test submission
        (job~17693158) demonstrated exactly this: it loaded the new
        ensemble classifier correctly but silently re-stamped a policy
        trained two days earlier under the old configuration, which would
        have looked, from the output alone, like a successful ensemble-guided
        training run. \file{checkpoints/n\_to\_p/} and
        \file{checkpoints/p\_to\_n/} --- along with
        \file{finetuning\_latest.pt} and the guidance-diagnostics artefacts
        --- were therefore moved, not deleted, to
        \file{checkpoints/archive\_pre\_ensemble\_default\_20260812/} before
        any real retraining was submitted, and a second smoke test (job
        17694467) confirmed 24 fresh episodes trained per direction with no
        resumable state found.
  \item \textbf{The eval pipeline defaults to the same shard directory
        the pre-existing comparisons depend on.} \texttt{run\_eval\_cf.slurm}
        writes \file{checkpoints/eval\_shards/} for the trained arm unless
        given an explicit tag, which is exactly the directory backing the
        paired significance test below and every previously reported
        trained-arm number. Regenerating with default settings would have
        silently overwritten it. Generation and reduction were tagged
        \texttt{ens\_default} throughout, writing to
        \file{checkpoints/eval\_shards\_ens\_default/} and
        \file{checkpoints\_ens\_default/} instead, leaving the original
        artefacts untouched for direct comparison.
\end{itemize}

With both directions' prior checkpoints archived, \texttt{run\_ppo.slurm}
(job~17694482) was resubmitted and trained both directions from scratch
under \texttt{USE\_GUIDANCE\_ENSEMBLE=True}: 800 episodes each, confirmed by
the domain banner (\texttt{ensemble=[densenet121-res224-rsna,
densenet121-res224-chex]}) and the loaded-model log line on every task, with
no ``resuming'' message in either. \ntop{} finished in 8h40m, \pton{} in
11h00m, both within the 22h wall-clock budget the job checkpoints against.

\section{What Changed on the Guidance-Side Metrics}

Re-evaluating the freshly trained policies
(\file{checkpoints\_ens\_default/eval\_metrics.json}, jobs~17695832/17695840)
gives a mixed picture on the metrics computed \emph{with} the guidance
model, which is expected: Section~\ref{sec:heldout} already established
these cannot be trusted as evidence of transfer, and ensemble CV asking two
classifiers to agree is a strictly harder bar than asking one.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Counterfactual Validity fell}, from mean $0.965$
        (single-classifier, Table~\ref{tab:results}) to mean $0.8375$
        ($\ntop{}=0.960$, $\pton{}=0.715$) --- consistent with a harder
        joint criterion, not a weaker policy.
  \item \textbf{Lesion Hit Rate rose}, to a $2.67\times$ lift over the
        radiologist-box chance baseline ($t=24.19$, $p=4.2\times10^{-53}$,
        $n=148$), above the single-classifier trained arm's $2.293\times$
        in Table~\ref{tab:lhr_arms}. Unlike CV, LHR is scored against
        ground truth, not against any classifier, so this is a real
        (if not yet significance-tested against the old arm) improvement
        in \emph{where} the edit lands.
  \item \textbf{Edit concentration stayed diffuse}: $0.253$ (\ntop{}) and
        $0.318$ (\pton{}) against the $0.05$ uniform baseline --- close to
        the values in Section~\ref{sec:heldout} and still far short of the
        $\sim\!0.5$ that would indicate a focal, lesion-shaped edit.
\end{itemize}

None of this answers the actual question. It motivates re-running the one
check that does.

\section{The Held-Out Transfer Result}
\label{sec:closing_transfer}

\texttt{arm\_transfer.py} was run against both shard sets directly ---
\file{checkpoints/eval\_shards\_ens\_default} (new, ensemble-trained) and
\file{checkpoints/eval\_shards} (old, single-classifier-trained, unchanged
since Chapter~\ref{ch:results}) --- scored by the same three validators used
throughout Chapter~\ref{ch:integrity}, \texttt{\{nih, pc, mimic\_ch\}}. This
set, not Table~\ref{tab:heldout}'s \texttt{\{chex, nih\}}, is the correct
comparison: \texttt{chex} is now a guidance-ensemble member for the new
policy, so scoring it as ``held out'' would let the new arm improve purely
by losing a validator rather than by editing more honestly, exactly the
mistake \file{arm\_transfer.py}'s own denominator discipline
(Section~\ref{sec:denominator_bug}) exists to prevent.

\textbf{It mostly worked.} Mean transfer rose on both directions ---
\pton{} very nearly doubled ($0.146 \to 0.293$), matching what the
no-policy ensemble sweep (Table~\ref{tab:ensemble_sweep}) predicted, now
confirmed with an actually-trained policy rather than the fixed default
sampling schedule that sweep used. Two of the three validators, \texttt{pc}
and \texttt{mimic\_ch}, improved on both directions.

\textbf{It did not work uniformly.} \texttt{nih} --- the toughest and most
independent of the three validators, sharing no training data with any
guidance-ensemble member --- improved on \pton{} ($0.100 \to 0.186$) but
\emph{worsened} on \ntop{} ($0.080 \to 0.046$), remaining flatly in
\file{arm\_transfer.py}'s \texttt{ADVERSARIAL} band on both counts. The
honest reading is not ``the adversarial gap is closed,'' but ``ensemble
guidance measurably narrows it against two of three independent
classifiers, while the hardest one shows no improvement in the direction
that matters least favourably for this claim.'' Section~\ref{sec:heldout}'s
central warning --- that a system's own validity number should not be
trusted without an independent, adversarially-chosen check --- applies with
undiminished force to this result too: \texttt{nih} was the check that did
not confirm the story the other two validators tell.

\section{The Trained-vs-Random Paired Significance Test}
\label{sec:closing_significance}

The second open item did not require new training: \file{paired\_arm\_test.py}
was run against the existing \texttt{trained} and \texttt{random} shard sets
that produced Table~\ref{tab:lhr_arms}'s control-arm row --- the same 183
images, single-classifier-guided, that Chapter~\ref{ch:integrity} left
untested at the per-image level.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption[Paired trained-vs-random significance test]{Paired per-image LHR
lift, trained vs.\ random, $n=183$ shared images (\pton{}).}
\label{tab:closing_significance}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Mean lift, trained & $2.293 \pm 0.780$ \\
Mean lift, random  & $2.204 \pm 0.772$ \\
Paired difference (95\% CI) & $+0.090$ $[+0.021,\ +0.160]$ \\
Paired $t$-test & $t=2.551$, $p=0.0116$ \\
Wilcoxon signed-rank & $p=0.0147$ \\
Cohen's $d$ (paired) & $0.189$ \\
Trained better on & $105/183$ images \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The gap survives: both the paired $t$-test and its non-parametric check
agree the trained policy edits closer to the radiologist's box than an
untrained one at the same action bounds and step budget, at $p<0.05$ by
either test. But \file{paired\_arm\_test.py}'s own printed reading is the
right one to close on: a Cohen's $d$ of $0.189$ against a between-image
standard deviation of $0.78$ is a \emph{small} effect. Eight hundred
episodes of PPO bought a real, statistically detectable improvement over
an untrained policy with the same action bounds --- not a large one.

Both items are closed in the sense that Section~\ref{sec:future} asked for
closure: a number now exists where an open question used to. Neither is
closed in the sense of settling the project's central claim. Ensemble
guidance is confirmed, with a trained policy and the correct leak-free
validator set, to be a real partial countermeasure --- and \texttt{nih}'s
\ntop{} result is confirmation that ``partial'' is the accurate word,
not a hedge. What remains, unchanged from Section~\ref{sec:future}, is
diagnosing \emph{why} the Grad-CAM constraint hurts rather than helps, and
whether a larger guidance ensemble (Table~\ref{tab:ensemble_sweep} suggests
three members) would close the \texttt{nih} gap specifically when actually
trained under it rather than only evaluated through the no-policy path.
The old single-classifier policies and their shards were archived, not
overwritten, specifically so that comparison remains reproducible.
