\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We use the RSNA Pneumonia Detection Challenge dataset~\cite{rsna2018} (DICOM, min--max normalized, resized to $256\times256$, binary label collapsed from bounding-box annotations). In the default configuration the guidance classifier is the mean-probability ensemble of two TorchXRayVision DenseNet-121 models (\texttt{densenet121-res224-rsna}, \texttt{densenet121-res224-chex}); the single-classifier arm uses \texttt{rsna} alone; segmentation uses TorchXRayVision PSPNet for lung and heart channels; a YOLOv8n feature extractor supplies the foreign-object embedding. Each policy is trained for 800 episodes per direction; for the 2-model ensemble run the two directions took 8h40m (n$\to$p, on an A100) and 11h00m (p$\to$n, on an H100), both including LoRA fine-tuning (5.7\,h and 5.1\,h).

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main-results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports the default configuration (2-model guidance ensemble, no Grad-CAM mask, no sparsity reward). The policy concentrates edit mass on radiologist-annotated lesions far above chance (2.60$\times$), and adds to that over the generator's default schedule without a policy, run on the same images with the same guidance, groups, adapters and seeds: lift 2.064 (paired gap $+0.532$, 95\% CI $[+0.423, +0.647]$, $d=0.764$, better on 128/148 images) and p$\to$n CV 0.225 (90 images gain validity, 1 loses it; McNemar $p=7.4\times10^{-26}$). In n$\to$p it adds nothing measurable (CV 0.945 vs.\ 0.940, 4 vs.\ 3 discordant). And yet the edits are diffuse within the lung. About 82\% of the difference mass falls inside the editable region (49--59\% of the image), but only a quarter to a third lies in the top 5\% of pixels, against 0.05 for uniform change. The 65\% of pixels changed is inflated by the VAE round trip, which alone changes about half of them; measured against $D(E(x))$ it is 41--44\% (Supp.~E6). The no-policy schedule scores lower FID and KID than the policy in both directions (FID bootstrap intervals do not overlap), but FID cannot rank arms: its extractor also guides generation. Clinical proxies support no clinical claim (Supp.~E2).

\paragraph{A fixed schedule matches the policy.} To ask whether the gain comes from per-image adaptation, we hold every control at the policy's mean action over its evaluation steps, with the same step budget and validity-based stopping and no training. On the same images this fixed schedule recovers 87\% (95\% CI $[71, 102]\%$) of the policy's LHR gain over the no-policy schedule and 98\% ($[86, 111]\%$) of its p$\to$n CV gain, and it is not distinguishable from the policy (LHR $-0.071$, $[-0.161, +0.019]$, $p=0.12$; p$\to$n CV 0.660 vs.\ 0.670, 14 vs.\ 16 discordant). In n$\to$p it is valid on exactly the same 189 images. Its mean p$\to$n transfer is lower (0.244 vs.\ 0.276). What the policy contributed here is an aggressive schedule, not per-image adaptation. \subsection{CV overstates what the edits achieve}
\label{sec:cv-overstates}

Table~\ref{tab:transfer-ratio} is consistent with this, but not decisive. Against the earlier single-classifier policy, the 2-model ensemble-guided policy has lower mean CV (0.808 vs.\ 0.940) but higher LHR lift (2.595 vs.\ 2.293) and higher 3-validator mean held-out transfer in both directions (Table~\ref{tab:five-arms}; same orderings under matched adapters); the one per-validator exception, \texttt{nih} n$\to$p, is not distinguishable from noise (below). The metric that fell is the one that shares a classifier with the generator. The two runs differ in guidance, in the classifier-selected groups and in hardware (\S\ref{sec:controls}), and with one training run per arm these differences are not separated from run-to-run variation (\S\ref{sec:five-arms}).

A single-run point estimate cannot resolve the apparent \texttt{nih} regression, so we bootstrap it (\S\ref{sec:transfer}). For n$\to$p, $[0.048, 0.114]$ (single) vs.\ $[0.005, 0.082]$ (ensemble): the intervals overlap substantially and the apparent worsening is not distinguishable from noise. Both sit entirely below 0.15, so what is established is that \texttt{nih} n$\to$p stays confidently low under either policy. For p$\to$n, $[0.075, 0.126]$ vs.\ $[0.138, 0.193]$: the intervals do not overlap (resampling within a single training run). We report each policy's interval separately rather than a difference-of-ratios bootstrap, because the classifier that sorts images into $X_n/X_p$ changed between runs and the two groups share only part of their members; a paired resample would synthesize a pairing that is not there.

The 0.15 cut-off is a conservative operating point, not a calibrated boundary, so we recount Table~\ref{tab:transfer-ratio}'s adversarial cells at other thresholds (point estimates, strictly below the threshold). \texttt{nih} n$\to$p is adversarial for both policies at every threshold from 0.10 to 0.25. Of the six cells, the single-classifier policy has 1/3/4/5 adversarial at 0.10/0.15/0.20/0.25 and the ensemble 1/1/2/2. Two cells sit on boundaries: the single-classifier \texttt{nih} p$\to$n ratio is 0.100 and so is not strictly below 0.10, and the ensemble's \texttt{nih} p$\to$n ratio of 0.165 crosses at 0.20, so reading it as non-adversarial holds only for thresholds up to 0.165.

This is the paper's central empirical claim. In n$\to$p the default policy reaches a CV of 0.945, yet the three held-out validators reproduce on average 28\% of the guidance shift and \texttt{nih} 4\%; the single-classifier policy shows the same pattern (CV 0.980, mean transfer 0.230, \texttt{nih} 0.081). In that direction the policies largely produce perturbations that other models do not see, rather than explanations of the pathology, and nothing in the conventional metric suite surfaces that.

\subsection{Five trained arms}
\label{sec:five-arms}

Every arm in Table~\ref{tab:five-arms} is a policy trained for 800 episodes per direction and evaluated end to end, with one training run per arm. All five share the split and seeds of \S\ref{sec:controls}. LHR verdicts are paired tests on the images two arms share, never differences of group means; transfer comparisons use each arm's own bootstrap CI (\S\ref{sec:cv-overstates}).

\paragraph{Ensemble vs.\ single-classifier guidance, and seed variation.} The two groups share 78 of 200 p$\to$n sources, 76 of them annotated (two shared sources carry no box). On those 76 images the paired LHR test favours the ensemble-guided policy ($+0.285$, 95\% CI $[+0.155, +0.418]$, $d=0.482$ under the matched adapter protocol; $+0.189$, $d=0.316$ against the per-direction evaluation). A second PPO seed of the default configuration, whose two directions share one adapter and were both resumed from checkpoints without saved random-number state, scores lower on the same 400 images: LHR lift 2.479 (paired $-0.117$, $[-0.227, -0.006]$) and p$\to$n CV 0.495 vs.\ 0.670. Seed-to-seed variation is thus of the same order as the single-vs.-ensemble gap, which we read as unreplicated. Without a policy (groups selected by label), ensemble guidance raises paired LHR lift over \texttt{rsna}-only guidance by $+0.069$, $+0.069$ and $+0.058$ across three evaluation seeds (all 95\% CIs excluding zero), a smaller effect that is not a replication of the trained comparison. Growing the ensemble to three members improves \texttt{pc} n$\to$p transfer from 0.465 to 0.623 with non-overlapping CIs, does not resolve on LHR ($n=12$), and lowers mean CV to 0.624.

\paragraph{The single-classifier policy beats a random one, but barely.} On its 183 annotated images, the single-classifier policy beats the random-action arm by $+0.090$ (95\% CI $[+0.021, +0.160]$, $p=0.012$, $d=0.189$, better on 105/183). The effect is real but small, and smaller than either negative result below.

\subsection{Two negative results}
\label{sec:negative-results}

\paragraph{An annealed sparsity reward fails at its own objective.} Edit concentration stays in the 0.21--0.31 band in every arm we measure. The sparsity term was built to address this and had been disabled throughout, leaving its annealing ramp inert. We enabled it and retrained both directions. In n$\to$p the validity safeguard (Supp.~Sec.~R1) froze $\lambda_{\text{sparsity}}$ at 0 before its ramp began, so only p$\to$n was trained with the term; the n$\to$p changes are run-to-run variation (CV 0.935 vs.\ 0.945). In p$\to$n, concentration moved by less than 0.02. Meanwhile, under the matched adapter protocol, a paired test on the 148 shared annotated images gives a lift gap of $+0.275$ favouring the arm \emph{without} it (95\% CI $[+0.188, +0.363]$, $p=4.7\times10^{-9}$, $d=0.512$), and p$\to$n transfer falls from 0.292 to 0.212 with non-overlapping CIs on two of three validators. Without a working spatial mask the term degrades to plain unweighted $L_1$, which discourages edit mass uniformly rather than off-target mass specifically. This is a plausible account of why it competes with precise editing while failing at its own goal.

\paragraph{A Grad-CAM spatial constraint collapses the base task.} In a policy-free sweep, constraining edits to a Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} region lowered LHR lift rather than raising it, so we diagnosed it directly. Scoring 263 member-image observations under the exact ensemble Grad-CAM path used in training contradicted the explanation on record: it is \texttt{chex}, not \texttt{rsna}, that degenerates (66/263, 25.1\% vs.\ 1/263), and it does so at \emph{low} predicted probability rather than near 0.5, consistent with output saturation zeroing the gradient. When \texttt{chex} degenerates the averaged mask falls back toward the other member alone, and CAM--lesion overlap falls with it (2.052, $n=127$ on clean episodes vs.\ 1.347, $n=23$ on partially degenerate ones). The remedy on record, a finer target layer, eliminates the degeneracy (0\%/0.4\%) but drops baseline CAM--lesion lift to 1.196, barely above chance.

A policy trained under that remedy moved in the same direction, by a larger margin: CV collapses from 0.808 to 0.0175 (7 of 400 counterfactuals valid), the paired LHR gap is $+0.960$ favouring the unconstrained arm (95\% CI $[+0.815, +1.108]$, $p=3.6\times10^{-26}$, $d=1.068$, better on 138/148 images), and transfer falls on all three validators. This is the largest paired effect we measure, against a mechanism introduced specifically to help: constraining edits to a finer, less confident CAM region makes the base task substantially harder, and every dependent metric degrades with it.
