\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Classifiers deployed on chest radiographs are typically audited with attribution maps, which indicate where evidence is concentrated but not what change would alter the prediction. Visual counterfactual explanations close this gap: given a query image $x$ with prediction $f(x)$ and a target class $c$, a generator produces $x_c$ that (i) is scored at $c$ by the classifier, (ii) remains a realistic radiograph, and (iii) differs from $x$ only in a minimal, class-relevant region, preserving anatomy, foreign objects and patient identity.

The standard success criterion, Counterfactual Validity (CV), is almost always computed with the same classifier whose gradients guided generation. It therefore cannot distinguish a class-relevant edit from an adversarial perturbation tuned to one model. This paper measures how much that matters for one system. We pair CV with two companion measurements, one anchored to human annotation (a Lesion Hit Rate against radiologist-drawn opacity boxes) and one to model independence (a held-out transfer ratio computed with classifiers that never guided generation), and apply them to a diffusion counterfactual generator we built. The concern is not hypothetical there: our system reaches 94.5\% CV in the normal-to-pneumonia direction while the held-out classifiers reproduce on average 28\% of the guidance shift. We have not re-evaluated other published generators, so we make no claim about how common this is; we claim only that nothing in the conventional metric suite would have surfaced it for ours.

The generator, PPO-CF, is the test case. It frames per-step guidance control as a sequential decision problem: a PPO agent sets the sampling schedule (strength, guidance scales, pass count, stopping) of a frozen, LoRA-adapted latent diffusion model, on the hypothesis that one hand-tuned schedule fits a heterogeneous test population poorly; FiRe~\cite{zeng2026fire} instead hand-designs adaptive guidance and early stopping (\S\ref{sec:related}). The agent never touches the diffusion weights. The evaluation also tests that hypothesis, and largely refutes it. In normal-to-pneumonia the learned policy ends 199 of 200 evaluation episodes after one step, with strength and guidance bias at their bounds and three passes on every step; in pneumonia-to-normal it averages 5.46 steps. A fixed schedule set to the policy's mean action recovers most of the policy's gain over the generator's default schedule (\S\ref{sec:main-results}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item An evaluation protocol that pairs CV with a lesion-anchored hit rate and a direction-specific held-out transfer ratio with a threshold analysis, together with the split-, seeding- and routing-level controls an arm comparison needs in order to mean anything (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).
    \item PPO-CF, a PPO-scheduled sampling controller around a frozen, LoRA-adapted Stable Diffusion generator with a soft, segmentation-gated background lock (\S\ref{sec:method}), used as the case study.
    \item Findings on that system (\S\ref{sec:experiments}): high CV with low held-out transfer; lesion targeting above the default schedule but largely matched by a fixed schedule; two negative results for mechanisms designed to localize edits, one of them the largest effect we measure; and single-run arm comparisons in which seed-to-seed variation is of the same order as the smaller gaps between arms.
\end{enumerate}
