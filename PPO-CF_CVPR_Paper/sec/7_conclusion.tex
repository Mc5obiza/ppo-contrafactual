\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated a PPO-scheduled, LoRA-adapted latent diffusion counterfactual generator for chest radiographs under a protocol that pairs classifier-scored validity with a lesion-annotation-anchored hit rate and a held-out transfer ratio. Under CV alone the system looks strong: 94.5\% of normal-to-pneumonia edits flip the guidance classifier. The anchored metrics qualify that in three ways. Held-out classifiers reproduce on average 28\% of the guidance shift in that direction. Pneumonia-to-normal edits do land on annotated opacities (2.60$\times$ the area null, against 2.06$\times$ without a policy), but a fixed schedule at the policy's mean action recovers most of that gain, so the learned schedule is close to a fixed, aggressive one. And edits stay diffuse in every configuration we trained; the two mechanisms designed to localize them both reduced lesion targeting, one by the largest effect in the study. Our evidence is one system on one dataset, with one training run per arm. Within it, CV computed with the guidance classifier would have overstated what the edits achieve, and an anchored companion metric (to human annotation, to model independence, or both) costs little and changes the conclusion.
