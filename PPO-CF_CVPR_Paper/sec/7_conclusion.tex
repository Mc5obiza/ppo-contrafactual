\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated a PPO-scheduled, LoRA-adapted latent diffusion counterfactual generator for chest radiographs under a protocol that pairs classifier-scored validity with an in-lung lesion hit rate and a held-out transfer ratio, each calibrated against explicit nulls. Under CV alone the system looks strong: 94.5\% of normal-to-pneumonia edits flip the guidance classifier. The anchored metrics qualify that in three ways. In that direction, held-out classifiers move no more than they do under a norm-matched attack on the guidance models, and an iterative Latent Shift baseline behaves the same way. Pneumonia-to-normal edits do target lesions, but weakly (1.17$\times$ within the lung, half of it generic), and a fixed schedule at the policy's mean action does as well as the learned controller. And calibration matters as much as the metric: a whole-image null inflated lesion targeting sevenfold and produced arm rankings that vanish within the lung. Our evidence is two generators on one dataset, with one training run per arm. Within it, CV computed with the guidance classifier overstates what the edits achieve, and anchored companion metrics are cheap, but informative only once their own nulls are checked.
