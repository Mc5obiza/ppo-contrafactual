\begin{abstract}
Visual counterfactual explanations are usually judged by Counterfactual Validity (CV): how often an edit flips the decision of the classifier that guided it. Because one model both steers and scores the edit, CV cannot separate a class-relevant change from an adversarial perturbation. We study this on chest radiographs with PPO-CF, a latent diffusion generator in which a PPO agent sets the sampling schedule around a frozen, LoRA-adapted backbone, and a soft background lock largely restricts edits to a dilated lung region. We pair CV with two anchored measurements, each calibrated against explicit nulls: an in-lung Lesion Hit Rate against radiologist-drawn opacity boxes, and a held-out transfer ratio that re-scores counterfactuals with three classifiers never used for guidance, bracketed by an attack floor and a real-image ceiling. On RSNA Pneumonia, normal-to-pneumonia counterfactuals reach 94.5\% CV, yet the held-out classifiers reproduce on average 28\% of the guidance shift, no more than an attack on the guidance ensemble with the same perturbation norm. Pneumonia-to-normal edits do target lesions, but weakly: within the lung they put 1.17$\times$ the box's area share of their mass on it, about half of which is explained by where lesions typically lie, and a fixed schedule at the policy's mean action matches the policy. A whole-image null would have reported 2.6$\times$, below the 3.2$\times$ a lesion-blind in-lung edit scores. An iterative Latent Shift baseline shows the same high-CV, low-transfer pattern. Judged by CV alone, both systems would look far more successful than the anchored measurements indicate.
\end{abstract}
