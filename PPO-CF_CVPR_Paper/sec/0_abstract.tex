\begin{abstract}
Visual counterfactual explanations are usually judged by Counterfactual Validity (CV): how often an edit flips the decision of the classifier that guided it. Because one model both steers and scores the edit, CV cannot separate a class-relevant change from an adversarial perturbation. We study this on chest radiographs with PPO-CF, a latent diffusion generator in which a PPO agent sets the sampling schedule (noise strength, guidance scales, refinement passes, stopping) around a frozen, LoRA-adapted backbone, and a soft background lock restricts edits to a dilated lung region. We pair CV with two anchored measurements: a Lesion Hit Rate (LHR) against radiologist-drawn opacity boxes, and a held-out transfer ratio that re-scores counterfactuals with three classifiers never used for guidance. On RSNA Pneumonia, normal-to-pneumonia counterfactuals reach 94.5\% CV, yet the held-out classifiers reproduce on average only 28\% of the guidance classifier's score shift, and the one trained on NIH labels only 4\%. Pneumonia-to-normal edits concentrate on annotated opacities (2.60$\times$ LHR lift over the area null, against 2.06$\times$ without a learned schedule), but a fixed schedule set to the policy's mean action recovers most of that gain, and edits stay diffuse in every configuration we train (at most a third of the change in the top 5\% of pixels). Two mechanisms designed to localize edits, an annealed sparsity reward and a Grad-CAM constraint, both reduce lesion targeting; the latter collapses validity to under 2\%. Judged by CV alone, our system would look far more successful than it is; anchored companion metrics should accompany CV whenever the guidance classifier computes it.
\end{abstract}
