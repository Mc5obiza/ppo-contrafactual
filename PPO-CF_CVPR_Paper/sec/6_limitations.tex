\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Edits remain diffuse.} No arm we trained moved edit concentration meaningfully; every one sits in the same 0.21--0.32 band regardless of what changed underneath it. Whether diffuseness is fixable at all within a gradient-guidance approach is open.
    \item \textbf{n$\to$p stays adversarial.} On the most independent validator, both policies sit confidently below the 0.15 threshold. The ensemble-guided policy is the better of the two, but that comparison is confounded (below); we cannot show the gap closes. The 0.15 threshold itself is not formally calibrated (\S\ref{sec:cv-overstates} reports counts from 0.10 to 0.25).
    \item \textbf{FOP is not computable offline}, so IP measures a related but not identical property, and SCP is a coarse proxy rather than a trained detector; in the committed configuration it is degenerate rather than merely coarse (\S\ref{sec:clinical-proxies}). % TODO: unverified, depends on SCP 0.19%
    \item \textbf{Sample size limits several verdicts}, including the 3-model ensemble's LHR comparison ($n=12$ shared annotated images) and FID, whose variance at $n \sim 200$ in a 1024-d feature space is high.
    \item \textbf{The single-classifier arm is not a matched control.} It predates the split and seeding controls of \S\ref{sec:controls} and was re-scored with corrected code, and every trained-arm comparison is a single training run per arm. It is also an earlier policy that probably differs from the ensemble arms in reward weights, $a_1$ range and background-lock anchoring (unverified; its training configuration was not recorded), so its gap to the ensemble-guided policy is confounded and is not evidence for ensembling alone. The shared generator's fine-tuning set may also overlap the evaluation images in every arm (\S\ref{sec:controls}).
    \item \textbf{Adaptivity is not measured.} Per-image action statistics for the reported policies were not preserved, so the degree to which their controls actually vary across images and steps is not measured here. An earlier version of the policy saturated its guidance-bias control at a near-constant value. The trained-vs-random comparison ($d = 0.189$, p$\to$n only, $n = 183$) covers only the earlier single-classifier policy, and no comparison against a tuned fixed schedule is available, so the contribution of the learned schedule itself is not isolated. The policy network was trained on at most $800 \times 25 = 20{,}000$ transitions per direction ($T = 25$ in the last recorded configuration), so it may be heavily over-parameterized for this budget, and its trunk and size are not recorded for the submitted runs.
    \item \textbf{Two independent policies} rather than one target-conditioned policy; the harder direction cannot borrow capacity from the easier one.
\end{itemize}
