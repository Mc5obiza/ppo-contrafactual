\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Edits remain diffuse.} No arm we trained moved edit concentration meaningfully; every one sits in the same 0.21--0.32 band regardless of what changed underneath it. Whether diffuseness is fixable at all within a gradient-guidance approach is open.
    \item \textbf{n$\to$p stays adversarial.} On the most independent validator, both policies sit confidently below the 0.15 threshold. The ensemble-guided policy is the better of the two, but that comparison is a single run per arm (below); we cannot show the gap closes. The 0.15 threshold itself is not formally calibrated (\S\ref{sec:cv-overstates} reports counts from 0.10 to 0.25).
    \item \textbf{FOP is not computable offline}, so IP measures a related but not identical property, and SCP is a coarse proxy rather than a trained detector; in the committed configuration it is degenerate rather than merely coarse (\S\ref{sec:clinical-proxies}).
    \item \textbf{Sample size limits several verdicts}, including the 3-model ensemble's p$\to$n results ($n=32$, 27 annotated) and LHR comparison ($n=12$ shared annotated images) and FID, whose variance at $n \sim 200$ in a 1024-d feature space is high.
    \item \textbf{One training run per arm.} The single-classifier and ensemble runs share split, seeds, reward weights, action ranges and anchoring (configuration identical except the guidance flag), but differ in guidance, in classifier-selected groups (78 of 200 p$\to$n sources shared) and in hardware, and no trained-arm gap has a run-to-run error bar. In the single-classifier, 3-model and sparsity arms, the p$\to$n policy is evaluated with the n$\to$p generator adapter rather than the one it was trained with (relative weight difference $1.18\times10^{-2}$ to $1.49\times10^{-3}$, 33 to 259 times below the committed arm's 0.385), whereas the committed arm uses its own (\S\ref{sec:directional-policies}), so their paired comparisons against the default also differ in adapter; the Grad-CAM arm's n$\to$p policy was trained for updates 1 to 49 under a different adapter from the one used at evaluation. Re-running the identical protocol on different hardware regenerates different images, so aggregate metrics move by small amounts (LHR lift $+0.016$, $[-0.045, +0.098]$, \S\ref{sec:directional-policies}).
Generator fine-tuning, PPO rollouts and the validity probe use only the 80\% training fold, and every evaluated image is in the 20\% test fold. The guidance classifiers and the FID extractor are pretrained models, and the \texttt{rsna} classifier was developed on RSNA data, so we cannot rule out that they have seen evaluation images.
    \item \textbf{Adaptivity differs by direction} (action statistics from a fresh evaluation replay on the same 200 images per direction, not from the saved counterfactuals). In n$\to$p, 199 of 200 episodes end after one step; strength sits at its lower bound and the pass count is three on every step, guidance bias is at its upper bound on 97.5\% of steps (at or above 1.295 on 96.5\%), and strength boost is near-constant but not at a bound, while the classifier and preservation scales, stop signal and ramp start vary between images; with one multi-step episode, no between-image variance share is reported. In p$\to$n the median episode is one step and the mean 5.46, 31 episodes reach the 25-step cap, and over the 71 episodes with more than one step 67 to 78\% of the action variance is between images. The policy beats the default schedule in p$\to$n (\S\ref{sec:main-results}), but the trained-vs-random test ($d = 0.189$, $n = 183$) covers only the single-classifier policy and no tuned fixed schedule is available, so the value of per-image adaptation is not isolated. The 37.9M-parameter policy network was trained on at most $800 \times 25 = 20{,}000$ transitions per direction, far fewer given the short episodes, so it may be heavily over-parameterized for this budget.
    \item \textbf{Two independent policies} rather than one target-conditioned policy; the harder direction cannot borrow capacity from the easier one.
    \item \textbf{Not measured:} convergence curves (two logged points per direction); a LoRA-rank ablation; Score-CAM or consensus masks; runtime and memory on fixed hardware; n$\to$p lesion targeting (LHR needs annotated sources); a tuned fixed schedule found without RL. The lock width was not tuned (exploratory check in Supp.~E4). % TODO(J12)
\end{itemize}
